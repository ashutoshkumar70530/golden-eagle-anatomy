# Golden Eagle Anatomy — Mission Challenge Bank v0.1

**Status:** Draft item bank; review every item against final approved visuals and sources before release.  
**Format:** 15 items total, five per mission. Each item is worth one point.  
**Scoring:** Pass a mission with 4/5 or better. Maximum three attempts per challenge; retain the best challenge score.

> These are draft question specifications, not final Storyline slide content. For image-based questions, use only a rights-cleared image with a marker placed and reviewed against the exact visible structure. Do not rely on a generated image to establish anatomical accuracy.

## Mission 1 — The Hunter’s Head

### M1-Q1 — Locate the bill
- **Objective:** Identify external structures.
- **Type:** Image-based selection.
- **Prompt:** “Select the bill.”
- **Answer:** The hooked structure at the front of the head.
- **Feedback:** “The bill is the hooked structure at the front of the head.”
- **Source:** Cornell Lab of Ornithology, Golden Eagle identification: https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Golden_Eagle/id
- **Asset/review:** Use a rights-cleared side-view portrait; confirm the hotspot does not cover adjacent structures.

### M1-Q2 — Locate the nape
- **Objective:** Identify external structures.
- **Type:** Image-based selection.
- **Prompt:** “Select the nape.”
- **Answer:** The back of the neck.
- **Feedback:** “The nape is the back of the neck. Golden-brown nape feathers are a characteristic visible feature of Golden Eagles.”
- **Source:** Cornell Lab of Ornithology, Golden Eagle overview and identification: https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Golden_Eagle
- **Asset/review:** Select a clear side or three-quarter view; verify the marker placement.

### M1-Q3 — Distinguish bill from nape
- **Objective:** Distinguish structures using visible evidence.
- **Type:** Multiple choice.
- **Prompt:** “Which structure is at the front of the head and has a strongly hooked shape?”
- **Options:** A. Bill; B. Nape; C. Rectrices; D. Hallux.
- **Correct:** A.
- **Feedback:** “The bill is at the front of the head. The nape is at the back of the neck; rectrices are tail feathers; the hallux is the first digit of the foot.”
- **Source:** Cornell Lab of Ornithology, Golden Eagle identification: https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Golden_Eagle/id

### M1-Q4 — Identify the nape in a description
- **Objective:** Explain visible features.
- **Type:** Multiple choice.
- **Prompt:** “A field guide describes golden-brown feathers at the back of the eagle’s neck. Which region is being described?”
- **Options:** A. Nape; B. Bill; C. Primary feathers; D. Talons.
- **Correct:** A.
- **Feedback:** “The nape is the back of the neck. The golden-brown feathers there help give the Golden Eagle its common name.”
- **Source:** Cornell Lab of Ornithology, Golden Eagle overview: https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Golden_Eagle

### M1-Q5 — Avoid over-interpreting a photograph
- **Objective:** Distinguish visible evidence from unsupported inference.
- **Type:** Multiple choice.
- **Prompt:** “A photograph clearly shows a Golden Eagle’s hooked bill. What can you conclude directly from this feature?”
- **Options:** A. The bird has a strongly hooked bill; B. The bird’s exact age is known; C. The bird’s sex is known; D. The bird’s health status is known.
- **Correct:** A.
- **Feedback:** “The visible bill shape supports identifying the hooked bill. A single exterior feature does not, by itself, establish exact age, sex, or health.”
- **Source:** Cornell Lab of Ornithology, Golden Eagle identification: https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Golden_Eagle/id

### M2-Q3 — Identify tail feathers
- **Objective:** Identify external structures and terminology.
- **Type:** Multiple choice.
- **Prompt:** “What is the anatomical term used for the large tail feathers?”
- **Options:** A. Rectrices; B. Remiges; C. Talons; D. Nape feathers.
- **Correct:** A.
- **Feedback:** “Rectrices are tail feathers. Remiges are flight feathers of the wing.”
- **Source:** U.S. Geological Survey, *Thinking about feathers: Adaptations of Golden Eagle rectrices*: https://www.usgs.gov/publications/thinking-about-feathers-adaptations-golden-eagle-rectrices
- **Review:** Confirm terminology against the selected course reference diagram.

### M2-Q4 — Interpret a soaring posture
- **Objective:** Explain a visible behavior/structure relationship.
- **Type:** Multiple choice.
- **Prompt:** “A Golden Eagle is soaring with its wings raised in a shallow V. Which statement is supported by the field identification reference?”
- **Options:** A. This is a commonly described soaring posture; B. It proves the bird is injured; C. It proves the bird is a juvenile; D. It identifies a specific subspecies.
- **Correct:** A.
- **Feedback:** “Golden Eagles are often seen soaring with their wings held in a slight V. The posture alone does not prove injury, age, or subspecies.”
- **Source:** Cornell Lab of Ornithology, Golden Eagle identification: https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Golden_Eagle/id

### M2-Q5 — Recognize limits of plumage-based inference
- **Objective:** Distinguish features using evidence.
- **Type:** Multiple choice.
- **Prompt:** “A young Golden Eagle may show white patches at the base of the tail and in the wings. Which conclusion is safest?”
- **Options:** A. The amount of white can vary among young birds; B. Every young bird has identical white patches; C. White patches prove the bird is injured; D. One wing photograph always determines exact age.
- **Correct:** A.
- **Feedback:** “White patches can be useful identification clues, but their amount varies. Do not infer exact age from one ambiguous feature alone.”
- **Source:** Cornell Lab of Ornithology, Golden Eagle overview and identification: https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Golden_Eagle
- **Review:** Keep age language introductory and aligned with the selected field guide.

## Mission 3 — The Capture System

### M3-Q1 — Distinguish digit and claw
- **Objective:** Distinguish external structures.
- **Type:** Image-based selection.
- **Prompt:** “Select the claw at the end of the digit.”
- **Answer:** The claw/talon identified in the approved foot image.
- **Feedback:** “The claw is the hard, curved structure at the end of the digit. In raptors, the claws are commonly called talons.”
- **Source candidate:** BSAVA Library, *Anatomy and physiology*: https://www.bsavalibrary.com/content/chapter/10.22233/9781910443101.chap5
- **Review:** This is a general avian anatomy source, not Golden Eagle-specific. Verify the label on the actual selected image.

### M3-Q2 — Identify the hallux
- **Objective:** Identify external structures.
- **Type:** Labelled diagram selection.
- **Prompt:** “Select the hallux in the reviewed foot diagram.”
- **Answer:** The first digit, typically directed backward in the depicted foot.
- **Feedback:** “The hallux is the first digit. Use the orientation shown in the approved foot view to distinguish it from the other digits.”
- **Source candidate:** Juniata College bird lab anatomy reference: https://jcsites.juniata.edu/faculty/yohn/birdlab.htm
- **Review:** Supplement with a specialist raptor source and verify the view before release.

### M3-Q3 — Identify feathered legs
- **Objective:** Identify a Golden Eagle-specific visible feature.
- **Type:** Multiple choice.
- **Prompt:** “Which description matches the Golden Eagle’s leg plumage?”
- **Options:** A. The legs are feathered down to the toes; B. The legs are always bare; C. Flight feathers cover the toes; D. Tail feathers grow from the foot.
- **Correct:** A.
- **Feedback:** “Golden Eagles have legs feathered down to the toes. Do not confuse leg feathers with the scaly skin of the toes and feet.”
- **Source:** Cornell Lab of Ornithology, Golden Eagle overview: https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Golden_Eagle
- **Asset/review:** Use a clear image showing the feathered tarsal region and toes.

### M3-Q4 — Relate digit to underlying bones
- **Objective:** Connect external anatomy to supporting skeletal anatomy.
- **Type:** Diagram-based multiple choice.
- **Prompt:** “In the reviewed comparison, what supports the bird’s digit beneath its external covering?”
- **Options:** A. Underlying bones; B. Flight feathers; C. Tail rectrices; D. The bill.
- **Correct:** A.
- **Feedback:** “The visible digit and the bones beneath it are different anatomical layers. The final diagram must be reviewed so the labels correspond to the correct bones.”
- **Source candidate:** BSAVA Library, *Anatomy and physiology*: https://www.bsavalibrary.com/content/chapter/10.22233/9781910443101.chap5
- **Review:** Require a qualified anatomy review of the chosen skeletal diagram before publication.

### M3-Q5 — Avoid confusing a talon with a whole foot
- **Objective:** Distinguish visually similar features.
- **Type:** Multiple choice.
- **Prompt:** “Which statement best distinguishes a talon from a digit?”
- **Options:** A. A talon is the claw; a digit is the toe structure bearing it; B. A talon is the whole leg; C. A digit is a tail feather; D. A talon is part of the bill.
- **Correct:** A.
- **Feedback:** “A talon is the claw; a digit is the toe structure. The two are related but are not the same anatomical feature.”
- **Source candidate:** BSAVA Library, *Anatomy and physiology*: https://www.bsavalibrary.com/content/chapter/10.22233/9781910443101.chap5
- **Review:** Use consistent plain-English wording and verify terminology with a raptor specialist source.
