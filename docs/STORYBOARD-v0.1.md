# Golden Eagle Anatomy — Storyboard v0.1

**Status:** First production draft; science/visual review required before release  
**Format:** Articulate Storyline 360, desktop-first, English  
**Narration:** Optional; all essential information appears on screen  
**Design concept:** Living Anatomy Lab — wildlife explorer tone

> Production note: Use licensed or original assets only. Do not use the source websites' images in the course until the specific image's license and redistribution rights have been checked. Hotspot coordinates depend on the final approved image and are intentionally not specified here.

## Visual system

- **Canvas:** Storyline widescreen 16:9.
- **Exploration screens:** Deep forest green / near-black background, restrained warm-gold accent, off-white text.
- **Reference / anatomy screens:** Neutral background or dark field with high-contrast labels and fine callout lines.
- **Challenge screens:** High contrast, minimal decoration, one clear task per screen.
- **Type hierarchy:** Large screen title; concise instruction; short explanatory copy; clear control labels.
- **Interaction standard:** Keyboard-operable controls, visible focus, no hover-only information, and a text alternative for image-only information.
- **Motion:** Purposeful reveals and gentle zooms only; avoid rapid movement and flashing.

## Screen-by-screen storyboard

### S01 — Course opening
**Purpose:** Establish the subject and invite learners into the experience.  
**On-screen copy:**
- Title: **GOLDEN EAGLE ANATOMY**
- Subtitle: *Enter the Living Anatomy Lab*
- Body: “Explore the structures that help a Golden Eagle see, fly, and capture prey.”
- Button: **BEGIN EXPLORATION**
**Visual:** Original/licensed Golden Eagle hero image, strong silhouette, restrained gold callout motif.  
**Interaction:** Begin button opens S02.  
**Accessibility:** Title and button remain readable without animation.

### S02 — How to explore
**Purpose:** Teach interaction conventions before the first anatomy task.  
**On-screen copy:**
- Heading: **Look closer. Find the structure. Learn its role.**
- “Select a marker to inspect a feature. Use the close-up control to zoom in. You can return to the full view at any time.”
- “You can complete this course without sound.”
- Button: **ENTER THE LAB**
**Visual:** Small demonstration using a generic non-anatomical marker illustration or an approved course visual.  
**Interaction:** Continue to S03.  
**Accessibility:** Explain keyboard equivalents in the final build.

### S03 — Mission hub
**Purpose:** Show the route and progress requirements.  
**On-screen copy:**
- Heading: **YOUR FIELD MISSIONS**
- Mission 1: **THE HUNTER’S HEAD** — Identify head structures.
- Mission 2: **THE FLIGHT SYSTEM** — Explore wings, feathers, tail, and body.
- Mission 3: **THE CAPTURE SYSTEM** — Explore feet, talons, and selected supporting bones.
- “Pass each mission challenge to unlock the next. Complete all three missions and pass the final assessment to finish.”
**Interaction:** Mission 1 available initially; later missions unlock after the prior mission is passed.  
**State:** Store mission status and best challenge score.

### S04 — Mission 1 briefing: The Hunter’s Head
**Purpose:** Set a focused observation task.  
**On-screen copy:**
- Heading: **MISSION 1 — THE HUNTER’S HEAD**
- “Start with the visible features. Look carefully at the bill and the surrounding head.”
- Goal: “Identify selected head structures and explain their basic roles.”
- Button: **INSPECT THE HEAD**
**Visual:** Approved close-up head portrait.  
**Science note:** A Golden Eagle has a strongly hooked bill; source: Cornell Lab of Ornithology Golden Eagle identification page.  
**Interaction:** Opens S05.

### S05 — Mission 1 exploration
**Purpose:** Guided recognition and vocabulary.  
**On-screen copy:**
- Heading: **READ THE FEATURES**
- Intro: “Select each marker to reveal its name and the approved explanation.”
- Label card 1: **BILL** — “The hooked bill is a prominent feature of this hunting bird.”  
- Label card 2: **NAPE** — “The nape is the back of the neck. Golden-brown feathers here help give the species its name.”
- Label card 3: **EYE REGION** — “Inspect the visible eye region; use the approved reference image for any detailed labels.”
**Visual:** Licensed/original portrait with accurately positioned callouts.  
**Interaction:** Hotspots reveal label cards; provide a visible list alternative to hotspot discovery.  
**Science review:** Verify exact visible structures and any additional bill parts before adding more labels. Do not add cere/nostril labels unless clearly visible and verified.

### S06 — Mission 1 challenge
**Purpose:** Assess recognition, function, and discrimination.  
**Instructions:** “Choose the structure named in each prompt. You have up to three attempts for this challenge.”  
**Items:** Five items from the approved Mission 1 item bank.  
**Interaction:** One item at a time; submit answer; immediate corrective feedback; retry if incorrect and attempts remain.  
**Scoring:** 1 point per correct item; pass at 4/5. Retain best attempt score.  
**State:** After three attempts without passing, show review and a clear next step; do not unlock Mission 2 automatically.

### S07 — Mission 1 result and review
**Purpose:** Reinforce learning and confirm progression.  
**On-screen copy if passed:** “MISSION COMPLETE. You can now recognize the head features covered in this mission.”  
**On-screen copy if not passed:** “Review the highlighted features, then use your remaining attempt if available.”  
**Interaction:** Review missed structures; if passed, unlock Mission 2.  
**State:** Keep best score and pass status separately.

### S08 — Mission 2 briefing: The Flight System
**Purpose:** Direct attention to wings, feathers, tail, and body.  
**On-screen copy:**
- Heading: **MISSION 2 — THE FLIGHT SYSTEM**
- “A Golden Eagle’s long wings and tail are easy to notice in flight. This mission focuses on recognizing the structures—not guessing from a silhouette alone.”
- Goal: “Identify selected wing and tail structures and connect them to basic flight roles.”
- Button: **EXPLORE THE WINGS**
**Visual:** Approved image showing wing shape and tail clearly.

### S09 — Mission 2 exploration
**Purpose:** Introduce visible wing and tail landmarks.  
**On-screen copy:**
- Heading: **WINGS, FEATHERS, AND TAIL**
- “Flight feathers include the primary and secondary feathers of the wing. The tail also carries long feathers called rectrices.”
- “Select a marker to inspect the approved label. If the image does not show a structure clearly, use the labelled reference view.”
**Visual:** Spread-wing / in-flight photo paired with a reviewed feather-group diagram.  
**Interaction:** Toggle between full view and labelled diagram; selectable callouts for wing region, primary feathers, secondary feathers, and tail/rectrices only where the visual supports accurate placement.  
**Science review:** Validate feather group labels with an ornithology reference before publication. Do not infer age from a single plumage patch; juvenile and immature patterns vary.

### S10 — Mission 2 challenge
**Purpose:** Assess structure identification and structure/function understanding.  
**Instructions:** “Use the view carefully. Select the best answer for each item.”  
**Items:** Five items from the approved Mission 2 item bank.  
**Scoring:** 1 point each; pass at 4/5; maximum three attempts; best score retained.  
**Feedback:** Identify the relevant region or feather group and give a concise explanation.

### S11 — Mission 2 result and review
**Purpose:** Reinforce recognition and manage progression.  
**Passed:** “MISSION COMPLETE. You can now distinguish the wing and tail structures covered here.”  
**Not passed:** “Review the labelled views and try again when an attempt remains.”  
**Interaction:** Review missed structures; passing unlocks Mission 3.

### S12 — Mission 3 briefing: The Capture System
**Purpose:** Focus on feet, digits, and talons.  
**On-screen copy:**
- Heading: **MISSION 3 — THE CAPTURE SYSTEM**
- “Look closely at the foot. The toes/digits and their claws are different structures.”
- Goal: “Identify selected foot structures and connect them to a verified supporting skeletal view.”
- Button: **INSPECT THE FOOT**
**Visual:** High-resolution foot close-up from a rights-cleared source.

### S13 — Mission 3 exploration
**Purpose:** Distinguish external foot anatomy from underlying skeletal structures.  
**On-screen copy:**
- Heading: **FOOT, DIGITS, AND TALONS**
- “A talon is the claw at the end of a digit. The Golden Eagle has a notably long, sharp rear claw (the hallux claw).”
- “Switch between the external view and the reviewed skeletal diagram. The two views explain different layers of anatomy.”
**Visual:** External foot photo plus separate reviewed skeletal illustration. Do not use an anatomically aligned overlay unless a qualified review confirms alignment.  
**Interaction:** Select foot, digit/toe, talon/claw, and hallux/rear digit labels on approved visuals.  
**Science review:** Confirm terminology and precise skeletal labels with a suitable anatomy source before including them.

### S14 — Mission 3 challenge
**Purpose:** Assess identification, terminology, and basic external-to-skeletal relationship.  
**Instructions:** “Identify the structure shown. Pay attention to the difference between a digit and its claw.”  
**Items:** Five items from the approved Mission 3 item bank.  
**Scoring:** 1 point each; pass at 4/5; maximum three attempts; best score retained.  
**Feedback:** Correct the specific confusion, using a text explanation and a visual highlight.

### S15 — Mission 3 result and review
**Purpose:** Confirm mission completion and unlock the assessment.  
**Passed:** “ALL THREE MISSIONS COMPLETE. Next, test what you can identify without guided labels.”  
**Not passed:** “Review the external and skeletal views, then retry if an attempt remains.”  
**Interaction:** Passing unlocks S16.

### S16 — Final assessment briefing
**Purpose:** Explain the summative assessment rules.  
**On-screen copy:**
- Heading: **FINAL FIELD CHECK**
- “Answer 10 questions covering all three missions.”
- “You need 7 correct answers to pass.”
- “You have up to three attempts at the assessment as a whole.”
- “Your highest assessment score is retained. Your pass status is tracked separately.”
- Button: **START ASSESSMENT**
**Accessibility:** Ensure the rules can be reviewed before the assessment begins.

### S17 — Final assessment
**Purpose:** Measure achievement of all four course objectives.  
**Structure:** Ten questions, one point each. Use a balanced mix of image-based identification, function understanding, and visual discrimination. Include one carefully reviewed external-to-skeletal relationship item.  
**Navigation:** One question per screen or a clearly navigable set; prevent accidental submission and communicate unanswered items.  
**Attempts:** Up to three complete assessment attempts.  
**Score retention:** Store the highest score across attempts; separately store pass status.  
**Feedback policy:** Recommended default—brief outcome feedback at submission and targeted review before a subsequent attempt. Confirm final feedback treatment in the Storyline implementation.

### S18 — Final results
**Purpose:** Communicate the result and next step.  
**Pass copy:** “FIELD CHECK PASSED. You completed all three missions and demonstrated the required knowledge of Golden Eagle anatomy.”  
**Not-passed copy with attempts remaining:** “Not passed yet. Review the recommended topics before your next attempt.”  
**Attempts exhausted:** “Your attempts are complete. Review the course material and follow the course’s available next-step instructions.”  
**Interaction:** Provide review access as permitted by assessment policy. Avoid claiming certification or professional qualification.

## Assessment item-bank specification (to be authored and verified)

Create 15 mission challenge items (5 per mission) and 10 final assessment items. The final assessment should sample all objectives, avoid reusing the exact challenge prompt where possible, and use plausible distractors without trick wording.

Recommended final-assessment coverage:
- 3 items: structure identification
- 3 items: basic structure/function
- 3 items: visual discrimination
- 1 item: external-to-skeletal relationship

Each item record must include: objective, mission, prompt, image asset ID (if applicable), answer options, correct answer, feedback/rationale, source reference, accessibility alternative, and reviewer status.
